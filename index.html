Kalimba — a seven-sided body with a septafoil rosette

← All projects

Read the README

Turn it in 3D

A kalimba is a lamellophone: tuned metal tines fixed over a bridge on a resonating body, plucked with the thumbs. This repository holds the body — a seven-sided box whose front carries a seven-fold knot rosette.

Seven and seven is the whole idea. The box has seven sides, and the rosette is a septafoil: one continuous ribbon crossing itself seven times, so the sound hole echoes the plan of the thing it is cut into.

The tines, bridge and tuning are not here. Nothing in this file is an instrument on its own.

The sheet

Click the picture to download the cut file. It is a display rendering — the cut file draws a hairline on no background at all, which a browser shows almost invisibly, so this is thickened and painted onto a light ground. Geometry and sheet position are untouched. The three lightest cut-order inks — green, orange and cyan — are darkened in these pictures. At full strength they fall below the contrast a light background can carry, so the cut order could not be read off them. Hue and sequence are unchanged, and the cut files keep the exact values.

What is on it

Measured out of the file, not copied from whatever drew it:

Colour Part Count Size
1 blue #0000ff rosette interlace and rim lines 1 49.5 mm band
2 green #00ff00 the rosette 1 48 mm across
2 green #00ff00 round hole in the plain face 1 15 mm
3 orange #ff8000 stiffeners 3 133.3 × 36.4 mm each
4 black #000000 face with the rosette 1 175.6 × 171.2 mm
4 black #000000 face with the round hole 1 175.6 × 171.2 mm
4 black #000000 side panels, finger-jointed 7 79.1 × 65.1 mm each

Sizes are the cut path itself, not its drawn outline — the path is what the laser follows. Seven sides, two faces. The sheet is 578 × 245 mm, with 3 mm round and between the parts, and millimetre-true — 1 user unit = 1 mm with a physical width/height — so it prints and cuts at real size. The faces and walls carry a 0.14 mm kerf: their outlines are grown by 0.07 mm, round at the outside corners, so the joints press-fit once the laser has taken its line. The holes, the rosette and the stiffeners are drawn true; none of them is a fit.

Colour is the order, and blue is not a cut

Run them blue, green, orange, black — engrave first, then cut outward from the detail to the outlines. Outlines last is the usual reason: once a cut frees a part, anything still to be cut inside it can move.

That sequence is shared by every LaserMadeMusic repository: blue engraves, then green → orange → cyan → black, with black always the cut that frees the part and violet meaning skip. This sheet uses four of the five — there is no cyan stage and nothing to skip.

The blue is an engrave. Those lines are the rosette's over/under interlace hints and the short continuations carrying the ribbon's edges across the rim. They run across the ribbon, so cutting them severs it and the rosette comes apart as it leaves the machine. Give them a score or engrave operation, or delete the layer.

Give every colour you keep an explicit operation. A per-colour job silently skips any colour you leave unmapped — leave the black unmapped and you will engrave a rosette and cut no parts.

The three orange stiffeners are optional

They glue underneath the top sheet of the heptagon to stiffen it, and the build stands up without them. Each is a slice of the box's inside heptagon, 36.4 mm deep from the wall it sits against, with that wall's two corners cut back 4.7 mm to clear the joints. Cut them if you want the face stronger — worth considering, since that face has the rosette cut out of it — and skip the orange stage entirely if you do not.

The rosette

One self-crossing ribbon with seven crossings, 48 mm across: the 2-lead 7-bight knot from knotwork-soundholes, its 60 mm cut file scaled to 48, turned so a bight points at every corner of the face, and set 5.5 mm off the face's centre towards one corner. It is a cut-out: the removed material is the open area and the ribbon is what stays, and the ribbon's peaks deliberately overrun the rim so the rosette fuses into the face rather than dropping out when the last cut closes. There is no continuous rim circle in the cut layer, and that is correct — adding one drops the rosette on the floor.

The knotwork sound holes repository documents this family in full and generates any coprime leads × bights, if you want a different fold count for a differently sided box.

Before you cut

Cut it in 3 mm Baltic birch plywood. That is what this is built in, and it is what the finger joints are sized for. Finger joints do not tolerate being cut in stock they were not sized for — too thin and they rattle, too thick and they will not go together — so if you substitute anything else, regenerate the sheet for it rather than scaling it: --ply= in the next section. If the fit comes out loose or tight on your laser, recut at its real kerf with --kerf= (the house measure is 0.13 mm; this sheet was cut at 0.14).

No acoustic claim is made. A kalimba body is a resonator; how it sounds depends on how well the box closes and on the tines, neither of which these files decide.

The rosette removes a good half of its disc from a face that also has to hold the box square. Consider that before choosing a thin material.

Changing it

kalimba.rs draws the sheet from a few numbers. Build it with cargo build --release (it expects the lasermade-tools and knotwork-soundholes checkouts beside this one), then give it any of them as flags — a dash for each underscore:

target/release/kalimba --ply=4 --kerf=0.13
parameter default what it is
ply 3 mm the ply
radius 90 mm the box's outside corner radius
height 65 mm the box's outside height, face to face
sides 7 sides of the box, and bights of the rosette; read only in the design
finger 2 a finger's width, in plies
space 2 the space between fingers, in plies
kerf 0.14 mm the laser's kerf: the box's outlines grow by half of it; not in the design
rosette_d 48 mm the rosette's rim, across
rosette_offset 5.5 mm the rosette's centre from the face's, towards a corner
hole_d 15 mm the plain face's round hole
hole_offset 7.3 mm the hole's centre from the face's, towards an edge
stiffeners 3 stiffeners on the sheet
stiffener_depth 36.4 mm a stiffener, from the wall it sits against to its long edge
stiffener_corner 4.7 mm how far each of its two wall-side corners is cut back, along each side

The box is RegularBox's geometry: the faces are the outside heptagon with a slot under every wall finger, and each wall stands on one edge with its fingers counted along the inside edge, finger and space plies wide, and its ends jointed for the corner — one end fingered, the other cut back with slots for the next wall's fingers. At the defaults every outline lies on Boxes.py's own to within 0.05 mm². sides must be odd: the rosette has a bight for every side, and a 2-lead knot needs an odd number of bights to be one strand. knotwork-soundholes ships the 3, 5 and 7.

Every run prints a validation block — fingers on every edge, the rosette and the hole clear of the walls, the rosette's openings counted, the stiffener's shape, the sheet fitting the 600 × 305 mm bed — and exits non-zero if any check fails.

SolidCraft design and STEP file

kalimba --solidcraft=DIR writes the box as a parametric SolidCraft design, assembled: the plain face on the bench, the seven walls on its edges, the rosette face on top, one body each. Every parameter above except the kerf and the stiffeners is a parameter of the design, and every point of every part is written from them, so changing ply, radius, height, finger, space or the rosette's and hole's sizes and places in SolidCraft moves the parts with them. The design is the parts as they come off the laser, so the kerf is not in it, and the stiffeners are not either: where they go is drawn nowhere. The number of sides and the finger counts (nb, nl) stay as the defaults made them, and the design says so in read-only parameters: a change that would give an edge a finger more or fewer needs the generator.

  • solidcraft/kalimba-septa-box.solidcraft.json — the design, as SolidCraft's commands
  • solidcraft/kalimba-septa-box.sc — SolidCraft's own file, built from it
  • step/kalimba-septa-box.step — the solids as STEP, for any CAD program, built from it
solidcraft-cli run solidcraft/kalimba-septa-box.solidcraft.json --save solidcraft/kalimba-septa-box.sc --out step/kalimba-septa-box.step

Checked against the generator body by body, at the defaults and with eight parameters moved, every volume within 2e-6. Each pair of neighbouring walls overlaps by 37 mm³ at the inside of their corner, 0.63 mm² over the wall's height: that is RegularBox's angled finger joint as drawn, and the faces' slots and the walls' fingers do not overlap at all.
